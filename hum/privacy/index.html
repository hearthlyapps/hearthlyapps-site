Privacy

Your recordings never leave your phone.

What Hum records, what it keeps, and what it does not collect. Last updated: October 9, 2026 (first release)

This policy explains what the Hum app ("Hum," "we," "the app") records, how it is used, and what choices you have. Hum measures the sound a household appliance makes and shows you how that measurement changes between recordings.

The short version

Recordings are analysed on your iPhone. No recording ever leaves your phone. There is no account and no sign-in. There is no usage tracking. Hum has no server, no analytics provider and no advertising network, so there is nowhere for your data to go even if we wanted it.

What Hum records

When you tap record, Hum captures twenty seconds of audio through your iPhone's microphone. It measures six things about that audio: how often a repeating impact occurs, how impulsive the sound is, its crest factor, its loudness, how much of its energy sits in harmonics of the impact rate, and how much sits in the frequency range Hum analyses.

Where that audio goes

Nowhere. The analysis runs entirely on your iPhone. Hum version 1.0 contains no networking code for sending audio, no server to send it to, and no option to upload. The recording is held in memory while it is analysed and is not written to disk.

What Hum keeps is the six measured numbers, a name you chose for the machine, and the date, stored in Hum's own folder on your device so the next recording can be compared with this one. That list never leaves your phone either.

What Hum does not collect

  • No account. No email address, name or phone number. There is nothing to sign in to.
  • No analytics. No usage tracking, no crash reporting, no advertising identifiers, no TelemetryDeck, no third-party SDK of any kind.
  • No location. Never requested.
  • No contacts, photos, calendar or health data. Never requested.
  • No payment details. If you buy the unlock, the App Store handles the transaction and Hum is told only whether you are entitled to it.

Microphone permission

Hum asks for microphone access the first time you record, because there is nothing to measure without it. If you decline, the rest of the app still works: the safety checklist and the bundled sample recordings do not need the microphone. iOS lets you revoke the permission at any time in Settings, under Privacy & Security, then Microphone.

Third parties

There are none. The only outside service involved is Apple's App Store, for the one-time purchase and for restoring it, under Apple's privacy policy. Hum's terms are Apple's standard Licensed Application End User License Agreement.

Deleting your data

Settings, then "Delete all recordings and readings", removes everything Hum holds, immediately and permanently. Deleting the app does the same. Because there is no account and no server, there is no copy anywhere else to request the deletion of, and no request to make to us.

Children

Hum is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of Hum ever sends audio anywhere, this page will say so before that version ships, and the app will ask before sending anything. The sentences on this page are generated from a single statement of the app's data behaviour in its source code, and a check in the repository fails if the app and this page stop agreeing, so this cannot quietly describe a version of Hum that no longer exists.
