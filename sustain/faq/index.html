GLP-1 & Muscle Loss: Frequently Asked Questions

Lose fat. Not muscle.

Does Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, or Zepbound cause muscle loss?

GLP-1 medications (semaglutide, brand names Ozempic and Wegovy; tirzepatide, brand names Mounjaro and Zepbound) work primarily by reducing appetite, which lowers total food and protein intake. When the body loses weight quickly on a calorie deficit, some of that loss is typically lean muscle mass, not just fat, unless protein intake and resistance activity are actively maintained. This is a known characteristic of rapid weight loss in general, not unique to GLP-1s specifically, but appetite suppression can make it harder to eat enough protein without deliberately tracking it.

How much protein do I need while taking a GLP-1 medication?

Protein needs during GLP-1 therapy are generally discussed in terms of body weight, and many clinicians and dietitians recommend higher protein intake than the general population during intentional weight loss, to help preserve lean muscle mass while losing fat. The right target varies by individual (current weight, activity level, kidney function, and other health factors), so this is not a specific number to follow without guidance.

Why is it harder to eat enough protein on a GLP-1 medication?

GLP-1 medications slow gastric emptying and reduce appetite signals, so people often feel full after eating much less than before. That makes it easy to under-eat protein specifically, since protein-dense foods (meat, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes) take up meal volume that a suppressed appetite may not accommodate, even when total calorie intake feels adequate.

How can I prevent muscle loss while losing weight on a GLP-1?

The two most commonly cited strategies are prioritizing protein intake at a level appropriate for your body and goals, and maintaining resistance or strength training activity during weight loss, since both are associated with better preservation of lean mass during a calorie deficit. Tracking protein intake (rather than estimating it) is a practical way to catch a shortfall before it becomes a pattern.

Does a dropping number on the scale mean I'm losing fat and not muscle?

Not necessarily. Total body weight on a scale doesn't distinguish between fat loss and muscle loss, so a steadily dropping number can look like straightforward progress while still including a meaningful amount of lean mass loss. Body composition tracking, or at minimum tracking protein intake as a proxy for muscle preservation effort, gives a more complete picture than weight alone.
